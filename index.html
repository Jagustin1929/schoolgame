Lock 02 // Careers Portal Code

Convert the portal's binary code into a normal number.

The careers portal stores its access digit as an 8-bit binary number — the same 1s and 0s every computer runs on. Each column is worth double the one before it. Wherever there's a 1, add that column's value. Wherever there's a 0, ignore it.

Lock 03 // Fake Recruiter

Which of these is actually a social engineering attack?

Social engineering is when someone manipulates you — not your device — into handing over info or access. It usually leans on one of three tricks: pretending to be someone with authority, creating fake urgency, or being overly friendly to lower your guard. Pick the one that's an actual attack.

AA local employer's HR team emails the careers advisor from their company address to confirm what time a student starts work experience.
BA classmate messages the group chat asking if anyone knows what to wear for their work experience placement.
CSomeone on a job-hunting site says they're a recruiter with a "guaranteed paid internship", says only two spots are left, and asks for a photo of your licence and your online banking login "to process you quickly".
DThe careers advisor announces a careers expo in the school newsletter and links to the official school page.
—
Need a hint?
No real employer needs your banking login to hire you. Look for the combo of a too-good offer, fake urgency, and a request for something private. The other three are just normal school and work stuff.
Lock 04 // Job Offer Text

Last one. SENTINEL intercepted a text too.

"Smishing" is phishing sent by text instead of email. Read the message, then answer the question.

Messages
From +44 7700 900412
Hi! We saw your resume online and you've been selected for a casual job at SkyHigh Retail: $850 a week for just 5 hours of work from home! To lock in your spot, pay a $40 uniform fee here: http://jobs-start.cc You must do this by 3:15 pm today or the position goes to someone else.

Question: Real employers pay you — they never charge you. What does this message ask for that no real employer would?

Need a hint?
Look at the part about what you have to do to "lock in your spot". Which way is the money supposed to flow when you get a job?
Every red flag in this message:
  • Pay-to-work: a real employer never charges you a fee (uniform, training, "processing") to start a job. This alone proves it's a scam.
  • Too good to be true: $850 a week for 5 hours of work from home isn't a real casual wage.
  • Unsolicited: you never applied — "we saw your resume online" is how scammers start.
  • Unknown sender: a random overseas mobile number (+44 is the UK), not a company contact.
  • Sketchy link: a generic domain (http://jobs-start.cc) with no https and no connection to any real business.
  • Fake urgency: "by 3:15 pm today or it goes to someone else" is meant to rush you.
